Room 4 · The Kitchen

Who's been in the kitchen?

Grammar: modals of deduction — must / might / can't
The House on Willow Lane

Victoria and Diana wanted a quiet Halloween weekend, so they rented an old house in a village through an app. It was strangely cheap. The owner, Mrs Grey, sent them the code for the door and then stopped answering messages.

The house was cold and dark. There were cobwebs in every corner, and the furniture was covered with white sheets. Only the kitchen was perfectly clean, as if somebody had just tidied it.

At midnight, the girls heard slow footsteps downstairs. The old floor creaked. Diana felt a shiver down her back. "We're the only people here," she whispered. "Aren't we?"

They went down to the kitchen. A candle flickered on the table. The table was set for three, and the tea in the third cup was still warm. Next to it was an old photo of two girls who looked exactly like Victoria and Diana. On the back somebody had written: "Vera and Dina, Willow Lane, 1926."

Then, very slowly, the third chair moved away from the table, as if someone invisible wanted to sit down.

How sure are you?

mustI'm almost 100% sure it's true.
might / couldIt's possible. Maybe.
can'tI'm almost 100% sure it's impossible.

Form: modal + infinitive. It must be the wind. She might be a witch. It can't be Aunt Rose.

The clues

Choose the best modal. Then say why — and argue if your partner disagrees!

Solve the mystery: Who sat down in the third chair? Who were Vera and Dina? Give your theory using must, might and can't, then decide together: should the girls stay or leave right now?

Strange signs

Open a card. What's going on? Why is this happening? Give at least two explanations: one with must or can't, one with might. Bonus: what would you do?
