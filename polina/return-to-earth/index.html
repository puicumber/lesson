Return to Earthon dying, soil and saying goodbye

✦ ✦ ✦

A designer asks a strange question: what if, when we die, we simply became soil? A real talk, a little macabre, and surprisingly gentle.

IBefore you listen

Say your answers out loud, about 30 seconds each.

  1. What usually happens to people's bodies after death where you grew up? Has it changed in your lifetime?
  2. Why do you think channels like Dark History are so popular? Why are we drawn to death and the macabre?
  3. The talk is called “When I die, recompose me.” Guess: what could recompose mean here?

IIListen and take notes

Listen once all the way through. Make notes under each heading, in English, short phrases only. Then listen a second time and fill the gaps. Finally, open Read transcript on the TED page and check yourself.

IIIThe vocabulary of endings

Choose the word that fits each sentence. One word in the bank is not needed.

VLast night's phrases, returning

Key word transformations. Use the word given, do not change it, and use 3 to 8 words.

VISpeak

  1. Would you choose to be composted? Why, or why not?
  2. Is turning a body into soil disrespectful, or rather beautiful? Argue both sides, then give your view.
  3. How should we make allowances for people who are grieving: at work, at university, among friends?

VIIWrite

Your university magazine wants articles on the theme “Should we rethink how we say goodbye?” Write the opening paragraph only (100–140 words). Hook the reader, then show your angle. Use at least two words from part III.
