Quick review

Adjectives and adverbs · p. 38

The cider tastes sharp. Sharp describes the cider after a linking verb. In She tasted the cider cautiously, cautiously describes the act of tasting. A puzzle is frustrating and a solver feels frustrated.

A most unusual find means a very unusual find. The most unusual find ranks it within a group. The young refers to a group. Use young people to refer to individuals.

Work hard means work with effort. Hardly work means do almost no work. Compare arrive late and haven’t been lately. A weekly show airs weekly. Use in a friendly way to describe how someone acts.

Frequency usually goes before a main verb (I rarely call), after be (I’m rarely home), or after a modal (I can usually go). In short answers, Yes, I usually do is natural. Compare a stone step with a stony path and the first two chapters with two chapters.

Watch spellings like truly, gently, happily, scientifically, publicly. Everyday speech allows forms like Go slow. The adjective crooked has two syllables.

Comparison patterns · pp. 39–40

Eva cycles more often than Noah gives Noah cycles less often than Eva. Keep the ranking when the subjects switch. For equality, use as often as or the same frequency as. In negatives, not as/so often as and not such a difficult climb as are possible.

Much/far/a lot cheaper modifies a comparative. Much too expensive means excessively expensive. By far the cheapest strengthens a superlative. No better means there was no improvement. Any better? asks whether there was one.

Quieter and quieter shows a trend. The longer we waited, the colder we got links changes. Twice as wide as compares measurements.

Use prefer walking to driving for a general preference and would prefer to walk rather than drive for a particular choice. Further details means additional details. Either farther or further can refer to literal distance. Say older than and my elder sister. Use more tired than angry to compare two qualities, similar to for resemblance, and as a guide for a role versus like a guide for similarity.

Adjective order and similes · p. 40

Usual order before a noun is opinion → size or weight → age → shape → colour → origin → material. Try a lovely small oval mirror and an old blue Italian bicycle. Two or three modifiers usually sound more natural than a long string.

As busy as a bee means very busy, as dry as a bone means completely dry, and as quick as lightning means extremely fast. Other fixed images include as cold as ice, as white as snow, as good as gold, as quiet as a lamb, as cunning as a fox, as easy as ABC, as poor as a church mouse, as mad as a hatter and as drunk as a lord. Some sound playful or old-fashioned today.

Transformations · p. 41

Use 3–8 words in the gap, including the unchanged key word. Count contractions as two words. Read the completed sentence to check tense, comparison direction, and details such as who received an action, what happened and where or when.

She gave us a brief smile becomes She smiled briefly at us. Keep at us. I’ve never read such a witty essay as this becomes This is the wittiest essay I have ever read. Keep the noun and relative clause. Twice the price and half the price describe the same relationship from opposite ends.
