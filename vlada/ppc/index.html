Homework · Present perfect simple & continuous

Still Growing

Some things started in the past and are still happening now, like a plant that's still growing. How long? Since when? Learn the rule, then grow your garden: every finished step plants something new.

Step 1

The rule

It started in the past and it's still true now. Which form do we use?

since 2022now I've been working here for four years / since 2022. (I started in 2022, and I still work here.)
have / has + been + -ingPresent perfect continuous with action verbs: learn, work, wait, play, rain, study… How long have you been learning English?
It's been raining since lunchtime.
have / has + past participlePresent perfect simple with non-action verbs: know, have (= own), like, love, want, believe, understand, belong… We've known each other for ten years.
We've been knowing each other…
a result you can seeA recent action that explains what we see now: present perfect continuous. Your eyes are red! — I've been chopping onions.
Why are you so hot? — I've been running.
for+ a period of timefor two weeks · for ages · for half an hour
since+ a point in timesince Monday · since 2019 · since I was a child
live & workBoth forms are OK, same meaning.I've lived here since 2010. = I've been living here since 2010.

⚠️ Don't use the present simple or present continuous with for / since: I am working here for two years. → I've been working here for two years.

Step 3

Action or non-action?

Can the verb go into the continuous form (been + -ing)? Action verbs can. Non-action verbs (feelings, thoughts, owning) usually can't.
