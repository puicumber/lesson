Adverbs at the Clinic

Dr Fang is back from his holiday, and his clinic is full of adverbs. Adjectives or adverbs, confusing pairs, comment adverbs and where to put them. About 20–25 minutes.

Step 1

Adjective or adverb?

adjectivedescribes a noun, and comes after linking verbs: be, look, seem, feel, sound, taste, smell, become.The patient looked nervous.
adverbdescribes a verb, an adjective or another adverb: how, how much.She looked carefully at the X-ray. It was surprisingly cheap.
no -ly adverbfriendly, lovely, lonely, silly are adjectives. Use "in a … way".She smiled in a friendly way.
Step 2

Tricky pairs

Some adverbs have two forms with different meanings: hard / hardly, late / lately, high / highly, near / nearly, most / mostly, free / freely.

Step 3

Confusing adverbs

at the moment / actuallyat the moment = now. actually = in fact, really (NOT «актуально»!).
especially / speciallyespecially = above all, particularly. specially = for a specific purpose.
ever / evenever = at any time (Have you ever…?). even = to emphasise something surprising.
in the end / at the endin the end = finally, after a lot of time or problems. at the end (of) = at the last part of something.
still / yetstill = something continues (often longer than expected). yet = something we expect to happen (questions and negatives, at the end).

Step 5

What kind of adverb?

Each type has its usual place in the sentence. First, learn the types: sort each adverb.

TypeExamplesUsual position
Mannercarefully, quietly, fastafter the verb, or after the object: She explained the procedure clearly. Never between the verb and its object.
Frequencyalways, usually, rarely, neverbefore the main verb, after be and auxiliaries: I usually start at 8. She is never late.
Degreevery, extremely, quite, fairly, absolutelybefore the adjective or adverb: extremely painful, quite slowly
Timeyesterday, soon, last weekat the end (or the start): I saw him yesterday. But already, still: mid position; yet: at the end.
Commentunfortunately, luckily, apparentlyat the start of the sentence: Luckily, the tooth was fine.
Certaintyprobably, definitely, certainlyafter be / auxiliaries, before the main verb, before won't / isn't: She'll probably come. She probably won't come.
Focuseven, only, justbefore the word they focus on: He didn't even say goodbye.
